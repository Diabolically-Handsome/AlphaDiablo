## 2. Three scientific conclusions

### 1. The speed-runner hypothesis is falsified (the original concern)
Option B, 25% paid at once: died 61/63, far from the speed-runner signature (≥90); the training descent
rate never collapsed (800 in total). **Paying the worker for descending does not create a speed runner;
it creates a careful miner.**

### 2. The price axis has been mapped across its full range; gate B is blocked not by money but by a skill wall
Measured per-level death risk (1280 episodes): h(1)=0.16 → h(2)=0.55 → h(3)=0.67, a geometric ratio of
1.53× per level. On levels with h>0.5, no rational risk pricing can solve it:
- Double dose (option D): depth exactly the same as option B (−0.99 vs −1.00): the linear axis is
  exhausted;
- Convex curve (option E): high escrow at depth amplifies the urge to stay alive (the more assets held in
  escrow, the less it dares to die); depth falls instead of rising, but it hammered the worker into the
  first farm master ever to pass gate C in full.
**Conclusion: L2→L3 is a skill wall. Lowering h takes combat skill at depth, and that skill takes a
curriculum of deep exposure; it cannot be bought with the pay slip.** (This echoes R13 from end to end:
first the classroom, then the wage, and now the curriculum.)

### 3. The curve itself "works", but it has to be used in the right place (the answer to the request for a usable curve)
The combination of the d^1.6 convex curve + the escrow valve is shown to be **safe** (no speed running, no
arbitrage) and **strong** (the gate C breakthrough). It stays in the toolbox as a legitimate component:
once the skill wall is down, it is the right pay structure for sending the worker to L4/L5.
