# R11 notes (2026-08-28 to 2026-08-30): kill-unlocked descent bonus and three trial runs

## Core survival mechanism: descent bonus unlocked by kills (adopted design, replaces the time lock)
- First proposal: pay the bonus once the agent has survived N steps on a newly reached level. Rejected in
  review: standing still on a shallow level with brainstem life support collects it for free (reward
  hacking); the D clause does not cover the bonus term, and a time lock is passively obtainable on
  shallow levels.
- Adopted design: the bonus for reaching a new level unlocks after K kills on that level; dying before
  the unlock forfeits it (escrow; `descend_vest_kills` in `python/diablogym/env.py`).
  Three benefits: AFK play is not viable / it forces a per-level check-in rhythm (the same shape as the
  game's own leveling) / gear value (E1) is activated indirectly (unlocking at depth needs combat power,
  which needs gear).
- Parameter sketch: start at K=3 (tuned in G0), optionally K(d)=2+ceil(d/3). If the agent cannot kill K
  monsters at depth, the bonus simply stops paying = an honest capability probe (a feature, not a
  defect).
- Companion changes: candidate death-discount tweak gamma 0.7->0.8; asynchronous collection (fixes the
  straggler problem at its root).

## Trial 1 final reading (2026-08-29) and trial 2 set-up
- v3 (K=3, full forfeit) exam reading: the depth pairing went to zero (-0.04/-0.02) and every metric was
  statistically equivalent to the anchor line. The lock was turned too far, the dive subsidy was taken
  back in full, and the policy went back to farming.
- Binary search framing: an unconditional bonus = the reckless-dive endpoint; K=3 = the no-dive endpoint;
  the real answer lies in between.
- Trial 2 (v3b): a single-variable step back, vest_kills 3->1, everything else verbatim as v3.
  Expectation: K=1 lowers the compound skill threshold a lot while keeping the anti-AFK meaning; if the
  policy still farms, the next axis is the forfeit share (full -> 50%) or the death discount.

## Trial 2 final reading (2026-08-30) and the dual-attractor diagnosis
- K=1 exam reading: same as K=3. The depth pairing went to zero and every metric was statistically
  equivalent to the anchor line. The mixed behaviour seen during training exploration (depth 3+ 36.5%)
  did not survive into the argmax policy.
- Three points fix the picture: an unconditional bonus = suicidal diving; any forfeit lock (K=1/3) =
  pure farming. There is no stable intermediate state between the two attractors. The root cause is not
  the manager's economy but the frozen worker's survival cliff at depth: the manager can afford
  recklessness, but it cannot buy safety.
- The kill lock itself is certified sound: the anti-suicide valve works, has no side effects and keeps
  gear opportunities intact, so it is kept as a safety component for any future economy.
- Suggested R12 direction (open decision at the time): the war on the manager side is won (the economy
  can steer the manager anywhere); the constraint has moved to the worker. Unfreeze the worker and
  re-educate it under the v2/v3 economy (repricing gear via E1, plus potion autonomy 4B, only bite now:
  only a worker that is still learning can turn gear and potions into survival).
  Or, short term: probe one more step with a partial forfeit (50%), although the dual-attractor
  structure predicts a fragile knife edge.

## Trial 3 (scale probe) final reading (2026-08-30 run, read the next day)
- The 24,576-step unguided run and the 12,288-step unguided run produced bit-identical exam sheets.
  Evidence: the npz sha differs and the weight maxdiff is 0.04, but argmax agreement on the [0,1]
  manifold is 100%, and both are constant policies: always issue action 1 (DIVE).
- The "not taught enough" hypothesis is formally dead: doubling training did not move the attractor at
  all.
- A deeper structural finding: under the R9 economy the policy collapsed to constant FARM, under the v2
  economy to constant DIVE. With this observation/reward structure, a three-action manager always
  degenerates to "the best constant command" and never learns state-dependent switching. The SMART
  probe had already hinted at this: reading the situation beats the best constant by a thin +5.6
  margin, which PPO at this scale cannot capture.
- The full argument for R12 route A closes: the worker cannot survive at depth -> no switching policy
  beats the best constant -> the manager has no incentive to learn switching. Teach the worker to
  survive first; only then does switching have value and the manager has something to learn. Teaching
  order: worker first, manager second.
